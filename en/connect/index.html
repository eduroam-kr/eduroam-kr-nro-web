Connecting

You connect with the account from your home institution. No guest account is issued at the site you visit.

Check your home institution's page first. The realm, the certificate values and the covered buildings differ per institution. Find it in the institution list

Connection settings

Network name (SSID)eduroam
SecurityWPA2/WPA3 Enterprise
EAP methodPEAP or TTLS (varies by institution)
Usernameusername@your-institution-domain — e.g. hong@example.ac.kr
PasswordYour home institution Wi-Fi password
Anonymous identityanonymous@your-institution-domain (only if the field exists)

Dropping @your-institution-domain breaks the login. That part decides which institution the request is routed to.

  • For Korean institutions the domain ends in .kr.
  • It may or may not match your campus email address. Do not assume it is your email.
  • Check your home institution's eduroam guide page for the exact value.

Install the CA certificate first

Do not connect without installing the CA certificate and checking the fingerprint. Skipping the check can put you on a rogue access point broadcasting the same eduroam name, and your username and password would go straight to the attacker. The same applies to choosing Do not validate or No CA certificate is required, or tapping Trust on a trust-on-first-use (TOFU) prompt without comparing the certificate with your institution's published values. Connecting without these checks is at your own risk.

The certificate file and its fingerprints are published by your home institution — not by the site you are visiting.

Do the first setup on your home campus if you can. Crowded places such as conference venues, airports and cafés are where rogue access points tend to appear.

Per-OS setup

Windows
  1. Download your institution's CA certificate and double-click it. Install Certificate → Current User → Place all certificates in the following store → Trusted Root Certification Authorities.
  2. Pick eduroam from the Wi-Fi list.
  3. Enter username@your-institution-domain and your password.
  4. On the certificate prompt choose Show certificate details and confirm the name and fingerprint against the published values. The Windows dialog shows SHA-1.
macOS · iOS · iPadOS
  1. macOS: double-click the CA certificate to add it to the login keychain in Keychain Access. Open it again, expand Trust and set When using this certificate to Always Trust.
  2. iOS/iPadOS: download it in Safari, install the profile under Settings → General → VPN & Device Management, then enable it under Settings → General → About → Certificate Trust Settings. Missing this last step leaves it untrusted.
  3. Pick eduroam from the Wi-Fi list and enter your account.
  4. On the certificate screen confirm the name and fingerprint, then tap Trust. macOS and iOS show SHA-256.
Android
  1. Install the CA certificate under Settings → Security → Encryption & credentials → Install a certificate → CA certificate. Menu names differ by vendor and version.
  2. Pick eduroam from the Wi-Fi list.
  3. Choose the EAP method and phase-2 as your institution documents, set CA certificate to the one you installed, and put the published server name in the Domain field.
  4. Enter your account and connect. Never choose Do not validate.
Linux (GNOME · KDE)
  1. Pick eduroam from the Wi-Fi list.
  2. Set security to WPA & WPA2 Enterprise (or WPA3 Enterprise).
  3. Fill in the authentication method, anonymous identity, domain and inner authentication as your institution documents.
  4. Set CA certificate to the file your home institution publishes, then enter your account.
  5. Never tick No CA certificate is required. It turns off server checking entirely.

After setup

  • Once it works on your home campus, the same settings work at other institutions in Korea and abroad.
  • When you change your home institution password, update the password saved for eduroam on every device. Retrying with the old one can lock your account.
  • When your institution announces a certificate change, follow its instructions. With the CA certificate pinned, you usually stay connected.

If you cannot connect, see Troubleshooting.

Security policy

  • Accounts are for the sole use of the account holder and must not be shared or transferred.
  • eduroam records authentication events. Logs are kept by both your home institution and the visited one, and are used only for troubleshooting and security incident response.
  • Port blocking and bandwidth limits vary. The policy of the institution you are visiting applies.
  • Copyright infringement, unauthorised access, malware distribution and other unlawful activity result in immediate disconnection and notification of your home institution.
